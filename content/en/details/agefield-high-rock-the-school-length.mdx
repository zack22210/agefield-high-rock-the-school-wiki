For players looking to dive into this early-2000s throwback, the **Agefield High: Rock the School length** for the main story is approximately **8 to 10 hours**. Developed by Refugium Games, this indie coming-of-age adventure focuses on a handcrafted experience rather than a massive, endless sandbox. While the primary narrative follows Sam’s journey through his final months of high school, the total playtime can vary depending on how much time you spend attending classes, exploring the town of Agefield, and completing side objectives.

## Main Story and Mission Breakdown

The narrative of *Agefield High: Rock the School* is structured around the "Rock the School" plan, a scheme hatched by Sam’s new friends, Kale and Axel. Their goal is to leave a lasting legacy as legends before graduation. 

The game features a specific number of scripted encounters to guide you through this plot:

*   **Main Missions:** 32 missions that drive the primary story forward.
*   **Endings:** There are two different endings based on the choices you make, specifically regarding the prom at the end of the game.
*   **Side Missions and Activities:** 15 additional side missions are available for players who want to earn extra cash or explore Sam’s relationships further.

Because the game includes two distinct endings, players looking to see every possible outcome may find their total playtime extending beyond the initial 10-hour mark.

## Gameplay Systems and Time Management

*Agefield High* utilizes a complex time and school system that dictates your daily routine. Unlike many open-world games where time is purely cosmetic, the schedule here impacts what you can do and where you are supposed to be.

### The School Schedule
On weekdays, Sam is expected to be in class until the afternoon. The game features five different lesson types:
1. English
2. Math
3. Geography
4. German
5. Music

Players have a choice: attend class to earn good grades and pocket money, or "rebel" by skipping class to explore the town or take on side missions. However, skipping class comes with risks, as you must avoid the watchful eyes of teachers and local law enforcement.

### Combat and Controls
The developers have noted that the gameplay is designed to be "simple and old-school." The combat system is intentionally straightforward, avoiding complex fifty-button combo systems in favor of accessible mechanics that mirror the era of games it draws inspiration from.

## The Open World of Agefield

While the game is "proudly indie" and does not attempt to compete with massive AAA sandboxes, it offers a handcrafted town with several distinct zones. The world is designed to be dense with personality rather than sprawling and empty.

| Area | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **High School Campus** | The central hub where classes, social interactions, and many main missions occur. |
| **Neighborhoods** | Two distinct residential areas where Sam and his peers live. |
| **Town Center** | The commercial heart of Agefield, containing various shops and mission hubs. |
| **Countryside** | The outskirts of town, offering more space for exploration and bike riding. |

## Customization and Economy

Progressing through the story and side missions allows you to earn cash, which can be spent at various shops throughout Agefield. While the game is not a deep RPG, it offers several ways to personalize Sam’s appearance and gear:

*   **Clothing and Accessories:** Buy new outfits to change Sam's look.
*   **Hairstyles and Tattoos:** Visit local shops to modify Sam’s physical appearance.
*   **Vehicles:** Purchase bikes to navigate the four main zones of the town more quickly.
*   **Items:** Shops also sell various miscellaneous items, including "dirty magazines," staying true to the raunchy teen comedy inspiration.

## Content and Tone

*Agefield High: Rock the School* is explicitly inspired by the raunchy teen comedies of the early 2000s, such as *American Pie*. Media outlets like IGN and GameSpot have noted its clear inspirations from titles like *Bully*, though it remains a standalone indie project.

The developers describe the content as "edgy" and "crude," featuring humor that some may consider outdated or offensive. The game includes:
*   Mild violence (without blood).
*   Sexual innuendo and partial nudity.
*   References to alcohol consumption and sensitive topics.
*   A single-player-only experience with no multiplayer components.

## Agefield High: Rock the School FAQ

### How many missions are in Agefield High: Rock the School?
The game features 32 main missions that comprise the primary "Rock the School" storyline. Additionally, there are 15 side missions and activities available for players to complete while exploring the town.

### Does the game have multiple endings?
Yes. According to the developer, there are two different endings. A pivotal choice occurs when prom arrives, which influences how Sam’s story concludes.

### Is Agefield High: Rock the School an RPG?
No. While you can customize Sam’s clothes, hair, and tattoos, and make choices regarding school attendance and the final prom, the developers explicitly state that this is a coming-of-age adventure game rather than a deep role-playing game.

### Can you play Agefield High: Rock the School with friends?
No. *Agefield High: Rock the School* is designed strictly as a single-player experience from beginning to end. There are no cooperative or competitive multiplayer modes.
